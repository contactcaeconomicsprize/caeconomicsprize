    01 — About

    Economics rooted
    in the region.

    The Central Asia Economics Prize is a free economics essay competition for high-school students aged 14–19.

    The inaugural 2026 edition welcomed students worldwide, with all essays required to engage directly with the economic context of Central Asia. Participants chose one of four economics questions and submitted an individual essay of no more than 1,200 words in English.

    The competition encourages students to develop evidence-based arguments, engage with competing perspectives, and apply economic reasoning to real problems.

    02 — 2026 Entry Archive

    The 2026
    entry requirements.

    The inaugural edition was open to high-school students aged 14–19 at the submission deadline. Students worldwide were eligible, with every essay required to engage directly with Central Asia.

    Central Asian focus

    One country, several countries, or the region as a whole.

    Central Asia primarily includes Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan. Students are not required to discuss all five countries.

    • Free entry
    • Individual submissions only
    • One essay per participant
    • English language only

    03 — 2026 Question Archive

    Four questions.
    Your argument.

    Participants chose one of the four official categories and answered its question. Each invited careful economic reasoning, credible evidence, and an independent response grounded in Central Asia.

    04 — 2026 Rules Archive

    Clear standards.
    Fair participation.

    Technology and authorship

    Technology may assist with mechanics and locating sources, but it may not do the participant's economic thinking or writing for them.

    05 — Judging

    A careful and
    independent process.

    Essays were anonymized for review. Following initial screening and preliminary review, twelve shortlisted essays were evaluated independently by the final judging panel. The 2026 judging process is complete.

    Assessment principles

    What strong essays
    demonstrate.

    Strong essays will not simply describe an economic issue. They should develop a clear argument, use relevant evidence, engage seriously with competing explanations or counterarguments, and reach a well-supported conclusion.

      08 — Academic connections

      From essay to
      further inquiry.

      One objective of CAEP is to help promising students interact with the wider economics community.

      Where possible, outstanding participants may be introduced to academics or policy professionals whose work relates to their interests. This may lead to discussion, feedback, reading recommendations, guidance on future writing, or potential future collaboration.

      CAEP does not guarantee internships, research positions, supervision, or long-term mentorship. Any further collaboration is voluntary and independent.
